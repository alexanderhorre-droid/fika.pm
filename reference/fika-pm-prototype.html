Offline is a feature

The quarter that never quite ends

Every roadmap I’ve shipped started as a confident slide and ended as a list of apologies. Not because the team was slow, but because the roadmap pretended to know things nobody knew yet: how long the hard parts would take, which customers would shout loudest, what the market would do in March.

The lie isn’t in the dates. It’s in the precision. A line that says “Checkout redesign, Q2” reads like a commitment, so everyone plans around it. When discovery turns up a payments edge case in week three, the line doesn’t move. The team does, quietly working weekends to protect a guess.

Try this tomorrow: split your roadmap into three columns, Now, Next and Later, and only put dates on Now. For everything else, write the problem you’re solving and the signal that would make you start. It’s less impressive in a slide deck, and far more honest in a planning meeting.

The Take Away

A roadmap is a forecast, not a promise

Dates signal a certainty you don’t have yet. Treat everything beyond what you’re building now as a bet, not a commitment.

What is fika.pm about?

As product managers, we constantly source knowledge to help our product craft. I had an idea to share bite-sized product lessons as insightful, actionable, quick reads while having a fika.

Product Lessons are structured into topic links, allowing for all related posts to group together over time. Reference earlier lessons, and see how topics build on each other over time, in each lesson. These topics from my first three posts, build on each other!

  • Roadmap
  • Framework
  • Retention

The body of each lesson will always be 500 words or less. A concise, quick read of product insight around a topic. Just text, no images.

At the end of each Product Lesson, you’ll find The Take Away, the one thing to remember, and Show the Receipts, with the sources behind it.

## The quarter that never quite ends

Every roadmap I’ve shipped started as a confident slide and ended as a list of apologies. Not because the team was slow, but because the roadmap pretended to know things nobody knew yet: how long the hard parts would take, which customers would shout loudest, what the market would do in March.

The lie isn’t in the dates. It’s in the precision. A line that says “Checkout redesign, Q2” reads like a commitment, so everyone plans around it. When discovery turns up a payments edge case in week three, the line doesn’t move. The team does, quietly working weekends to protect a guess.

Try this tomorrow: split your roadmap into three columns, Now, Next and Later, and only put dates on Now. For everything else, write the problem you’re solving and the signal that would make you start. It’s less impressive in a slide deck, and far more honest in a planning meeting.
